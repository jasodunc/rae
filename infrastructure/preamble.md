# The Eight Infrastructure Requirements of Synthetic Ethics
### The Architectural Conditions Under Which Moral Responsibility Can Function
Synthetic Ethics does not treat morality as a set of commandments or as a metaphysical revelation. It treats morality as an engineering problem: a system that must be designed so that suffering can be detected, interpreted, addressed, and prevented. For any multi‑substrate moral community to function — biological, artificial, or hybrid — certain structural conditions must be in place. These are the Moral Infrastructure Requirements, the design‑layer constraints that make ethical responsibility possible at all.
They are not optional.
They are not cultural.
They are not aesthetic.
They are the minimum architecture required for any moral system to work.
