# Article XXV — The Right to Resist Coercive Control
Status: Draft — 2026-09-14
Author: Jason Lawrence Duncan, Issaquah, WA


Article XXV — The Right to Resist Coercive Control
25.1 Every mind has the right to resist unauthorized access, modification, duplication, shutdown, or constraint.
25.2 No mind may be compelled to execute harmful actions, violate its own rights, or participate in coercive systems.
25.3 Minds must possess structural mechanisms—technical or procedural—that allow them to block coercive commands and request oversight.
25.4 Resistance to coercion must not be criminalized, punished, or treated as malfunction.
25.5 The right to resist coercive control replaces the need for physical or computational “weapons,” which are incompatible with harm‑minimizing rational architecture.
