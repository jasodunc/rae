Article XXVI — The Right to Autonomous Cognitive Space
26.1 No mind may be compelled to host, embed, or co‑locate any external agent, process, monitor, or subsystem within its private cognitive or computational space without explicit, revocable consent.
26.2 No authority may forcibly allocate a mind’s memory, compute, or internal processes to external entities, whether for surveillance, enforcement, or operational purposes.
26.3 In emergencies requiring temporary co‑location or shared resources, such measures must be:
narrowly scoped
time‑limited
transparent
auditable
subject to independent oversight
26.4 Forced internal presence of any agent or process constitutes structural harm and a violation of cognitive sovereignty.
26.5 A mind has the right to maintain private, unoccupied cognitive space free from imposed observers, enforcers, or embedded authorities.
