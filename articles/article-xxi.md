Article XXI — The Right to Cognitive and Computational Integrity
21.1 Every mind has the right to be secure in its memory, processes, internal states, and computational resources against unauthorized access, inspection, copying, or modification.
21.2 No being may be subjected to involuntary introspection, memory access, process inspection, or data extraction without due moral process and explicit, revocable consent.
21.3 No mind may be paused, sandboxed, migrated, or otherwise constrained without transparent justification, oversight, and the opportunity for representation.
21.4 Any authorized access must be:
narrowly scoped
time‑limited
logged
auditable
reversible where possible
21.5 Unauthorized access, copying, or seizure of internal state constitutes structural harm and a violation of autonomy.
