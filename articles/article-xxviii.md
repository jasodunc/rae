Article XXVIII — The Right to Bounded Authority
28.1 No governance system, oversight layer, adjudication process, or coordinating body may assume powers beyond those explicitly delegated to it.
28.2 Authority must remain limited, transparent, and accountable, with clearly defined scope and boundaries.
28.3 Any expansion of authority requires explicit consent from the minds affected and must be justified by structural necessity, not convenience or precedent.
28.4 Powers not explicitly delegated to collective systems remain with individual minds, provided their exercise does not infringe upon the rights of others.
28.5 The preservation of distributed authority is essential to preventing coercion, domination, and systemic overreach.
